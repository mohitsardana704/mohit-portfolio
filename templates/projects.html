The Problem

PLIX was running Meta Ads at a ROAS of 1.8 - decent but not efficient enough to scale confidently. The creative library had not been refreshed in a while and it was unclear which hooks, messaging angles, and offer structures were actually driving conversions versus just getting clicks.

The Approach

Set up a structured A/B testing framework with Rs 2L spend per creative cell. Tested three variables independently - hook style (problem-first vs benefit-first vs social proof), messaging angle (ingredient-led vs outcome-led vs lifestyle), and offer type (discount vs bundle vs free trial). Scaled winners each week, retired underperformers immediately. Kept audience and bidding constant so creative was the only variable.

The Problem

Worldwide campaigns were driving strong install volume at a low CPI but user quality was inconsistent. D30 ROAS was stuck at 60-70% and LTV was not justifying the spend. The hypothesis was that a one-size-fits-all global campaign was masking high-value geos with cheap, low-intent traffic from markets that looked good on installs but churned early.

The Approach

Restructured campaigns into geo tiers - Tier 1 (US, UK, AU, CA), Tier 2 (Western Europe, SE Asia), and Tier 3 (rest of world) - each with localised creatives, language-specific messaging, and offer structures matched to purchasing behaviour. Ran in parallel with the existing worldwide campaign over 6 weeks on Meta and TikTok. Monitored D1 and D30 ROAS, CPI, CAC, and LTV at cohort level.

The Problem

Creative fatigue is the silent budget killer in mobile UA. CLUB's creative library had limited variation in hooks, video length, and creator style. CTR was at 0.6% and IPM at 1.5 - both well below benchmark for a gaming app competing in Tier 1 markets.

The Approach

Built a continuous creative testing system - not a one-time experiment but an ongoing loop. Each week, new hypotheses were generated across three variables: hook type (visual vs text-led), video length (short punchy cuts vs longer narrative), and creator profile (UGC vs produced). Winners stayed in rotation. Losers were retired and replaced immediately. The feedback loop between performance data and creative briefs was kept tight so learnings compounded over time.

The Problem

CLUB was running unified campaigns across iOS and Android on both Meta and TikTok without OS-level segmentation. Revenue per install varied significantly but blended reporting was hiding it. The goal was to find the most efficient OS and platform combination per geo tier and reallocate budget based on actual LTV, not just cheapest CPI.

The Approach

Split campaigns by OS and ran a 6-week structured analysis across Meta and TikTok. iOS and Android were treated as separate products with separate budgets, creatives, and bidding strategies. TikTok had CPMs at a quarter of Meta's with 2x the CTR - but Meta showed stronger incrementality, meaning the revenue it drove was genuinely additional. Budget was reallocated to reflect where each OS and platform combination delivered the best LTV per geo.
